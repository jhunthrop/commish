# Model v3: the features that separate teammates, and the news pass

**Measured 2026-08-28.** Reproduce with `npm run projections:backfill 2020 2024`,
`npm run projections:context 2020 2024`, `npm run projections:fit`, then
`npm run projections:ablate <group>` for any of `red-zone`, `target-depth`, `news`, and
`npm run projections:ablate -- news 2024 2023 --at=game-week` for the second replay moment.
Previous: `docs/research/2026-08-27-model-v2.md`. Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-27-projection-engine-phase3-4.md`.

Model v2 ended with a prediction, written down so it could be checked: *"the features that will move
this scoreboard are the ones that separate teammates from each other. Red-zone share, route
participation, target depth, and the injury and inactive designations that decide who is on the
field at all."*

Three of those four were built and measured here. **The prediction is half right, and the half that
is right is not the half it named first.** Red-zone share and target depth separate teammates
exactly as claimed and move the scoreboard barely at all. The injury designations move the two bands
where a manager's decision lives, on both held-out seasons — and cannot be reached by the replay
moment the engine currently uses.

| red-zone leverage | 0.6774 | 0.6778 | 4.574 | 4.462 | **no** |
| depth of target | 0.6770 | 0.6780 | 4.577 | 4.460 | **no** |

Nothing here ships. Two of the three groups earned an honest negative, and the third earned
something more interesting than either.

## 1. Red-zone share — NOT SHIPPED

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6774` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6778` (2023).

| band | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| elite | 0.0694 → 0.0544 (**−0.0150**) | 0.1649 → 0.1595 (**−0.0054**) |
| starter | 0.1750 → 0.1801 (+0.0051) | 0.2209 → 0.2164 (−0.0045) |
| deep | 0.3513 → 0.3559 (+0.0046) | 0.3221 → 0.3247 (+0.0026) |
| fringe | 0.2495 → 0.2508 (+0.0013) | 0.2763 → 0.2836 (+0.0073) |

Opposite signs overall, and the elite band worse on both seasons. It does not ship.

**Why the model cannot already see this, which is the good half of the argument.** Layer 2's fit
regresses rushing and passing touchdown rate ENTIRELY to the position mean — 10,000 opportunities of
prior, which `fitted.ts` prints as "no measurable spread between players". That is the correct
answer to the question layer 2 asked: touchdowns are too rare for a player's own touchdown rate to
carry information. It is the wrong answer to the question a manager asks, because two backs who
split carries evenly are not the same player if only one of them is on the field at the two-yard
line. Red-zone touches are roughly ten times commoner than touchdowns, so the same role difference
should show up in a tenth of the sample.

**Why it is small anyway, measured rather than guessed.** On the fit seasons, sorted into quintiles
by prior red-zone leverage:

| quintile | mean leverage | touchdowns per expected |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.607 | 0.859 |
| 2 | 0.850 | 1.004 |
| 3 | 0.997 | 0.938 |
| 4 | 1.184 | 1.056 |
| 5 | 1.566 | 1.126 |

Monotone at the ends and noisy in the middle: a 2.6x range of leverage buys a 31% range of scoring
rate. Two mechanisms cap it, and both are worth writing down because they are properties of football
rather than of this implementation:

1. **A back's inside-ten carry rate is depressed by his own success.** A forty-yard touchdown run
   from midfield never becomes a goal-line carry, and a back who is stuffed three times at the three
   accumulates inside-ten carries while scoring nothing. The statistic is negatively coupled to the
   outcome it is being asked to predict.
2. **A red-zone role is a coaching decision and coaching decisions change.** The fit chose a recency
   decay of 0.95 a week over a flat mean, which says the same thing quantitatively.

The elite band degrading on both seasons is the sharpest signal in the table. Those are the players
whose touchdown expectation is already large, so a multiplier on it moves their projected points
more than anyone else's — and it moves them wrongly more often than rightly.

## 2. Depth of target — NOT SHIPPED

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6770` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6780` (2023).

| band | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| elite | 0.0694 → 0.0655 (−0.0039) | 0.1649 → 0.1655 (+0.0006) |
| starter | 0.1750 → 0.1741 (−0.0009) | 0.2209 → 0.2230 (+0.0021) |
| deep | 0.3513 → 0.3547 (+0.0034) | 0.3221 → 0.3228 (+0.0008) |
| fringe | 0.2495 → 0.2531 (+0.0036) | 0.2763 → 0.2805 (+0.0042) |

Deep and fringe improve on both seasons; elite and starter are mixed; the pooled figure does not
move to four decimal places on either season. Six of eight is the count game context shipped on in
Phase 3, but game context also cut mean absolute error on both seasons and this does not.

**The elasticities come out with the signs the theory demands**, which is the part worth keeping:
**+0.165** on receiving yards per target and **−0.071** on receptions per target. A deeper target is
worth more yards and is caught less often, measured rather than asserted. A feature that had moved
only the yards would have been claiming free points, and the fact that both signs are right is the
evidence that the quantity is describing football.

**Why it is worth about one per cent.** The elasticities are fitted against the RESIDUAL of layer
2's own prediction, replayed week by week. Layer 2 already learns a receiver's own yards per target
with 218 targets of prior, and yards per target is mostly a consequence of how deep he is thrown to
— so by the time depth is asked what it ADDS, most of it has already been counted. Fitting against
the raw outcome instead would have found a large elasticity and produced a feature that measured
beautifully by counting one effect twice. That is the trap this whole harness exists to catch, and
it is the same trap the scheme group documented in Phase 3.

**What was not tried and should be.** The residual is small ON AVERAGE, weighted by volume, and the
players who dominate that weighting are exactly the ones whose own yards-per-target history layer 2
already trusts. For a receiver with thirty career targets, layer 2 has almost nothing and depth of
target has everything. A segment ablation restricted to thin histories is the obvious next
measurement, and this harness cannot currently express it.

## 3. Route participation — NOT BUILT, and the reason is a source

Model v2 named route participation beside red-zone share. It is not here.

nflverse publishes `pbp_participation`, which carries `offense_players` — the eleven men on the
field for every play, by gsis id, for 2016 through 2024. From it, "pass plays this man was on the
field for" is derivable, and targets per route opportunity separates a blocking tight end from a
receiving one in a way snap share cannot. The `route` column in the same file is empty for 2024,
so the derivation would have to be the participation one rather than the published one.

It was scoped and not built: it is a fifty-megabyte download per season on top of the hundred already
being streamed, and the two features ahead of it in the queue both measured near zero. Recording it
here as reachable, costed and deferred is more useful than building a third teammate-separating
feature to find out it is also worth 0.003.

## 4. The news pass — the contract, and a result the replay moment cannot use

### The contract, which matters more than the feature

The spec's rule is that the model **weighs retrieved facts and never generates them**. Phase 4's
plan put the contract tests before the implementation, and they are the deliverable:

- **Structured, never prose.** There is no free-text field anywhere in this pipeline. The store holds
  numbers, the retrieval maps four published words to four integers, and nothing between the feed and
  the scoreboard can express a sentence. A layer that cannot write prose cannot invent a fact.
- **Cited, or refused.** Every adjustment carries the feed, the metric, the season, the week and the
  moment the publisher filed it. An adjustment with no citation, or with a citation naming no source,
  or with an unreadable filing time, is rejected rather than warned about.
- **Confident in proportion to its evidence.** The confidence is in `(0, 1]` and pulls the multiplier
  toward doing nothing. It is fitted from the number of player-weeks behind each designation, not
  chosen.
- **Expiring.** Ten days after filing, an adjustment stops applying. A report is published about one
  game; stale evidence that keeps applying is how a returning starter stays benched forever.
- **A bounded multiplier on opportunity, and nothing else.** Never a component value, never an
  efficiency, never a team volume. A man who is hurt gets fewer snaps; he does not become worse at
  catching. The bound is `[0, 1.2]` and is asymmetric on purpose: zero is a legitimate floor, because
  a man ruled out takes no snaps at all, while ruling somebody's backup UP is an inference rather
  than a published fact and gets almost no room.

Thirty-three tests, and the ones that matter are the refusals: an uncited adjustment is rejected, an
expired one stops applying, an out-of-bounds multiplier is refused, and expiry is judged against the
moment being projected rather than against the wall clock — so a backtest of October 2023 applies
exactly the adjustments that were live in October 2023.

### The retrieval, and a departure from the plan

The plan named Tank01's news endpoint. Tank01 publishes what is happening now and keeps no archive,
so a layer built on it could ship and could never be measured — and this phase exists precisely to
catch a layer that feels smart and adds nothing. We retrieve the **official injury report** instead:
the same class of fact (a named publisher, a timestamp, one player's availability), archived by
nflverse back to 2009 with the league's own filing time on every row.

This is the same departure Phase 3 made with the play-caller table, for the same reason: a source we
can point at beats a source the plan named but nobody can verify. The contract is indifferent to
which feed fills it, so a live wire drops in beside this one without a line of change.

### What a designation is worth, measured

Everybody knows "Out" means no snaps. Nobody knows what "Questionable" means. Measured against layer
1's own prediction, over the fit seasons:

| designation | multiplier | player-weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Out | **0.004** | 610 |
| Doubtful | **0.000** | 109 |
| Questionable | **0.676** | 884 |
| Did not practise (no game status) | 0.792 | 218 |
| Limited in practice | 0.935 | 264 |
| Full participation | 0.959 | 1,223 |

Out and Doubtful are the sanity check: the measurement recovers what everyone already knows, from
data, which is how you find out the pipeline is wired up correctly. Questionable is the number
nobody had: **a questionable player takes about two thirds of the snaps the model expected of him.**

One methodological note, because it is the difference between this table and a comfortable useless
one. A missing player-week counts as ZERO here and nowhere else in the engine. Everywhere else an
absent player-week means "no evidence" and is skipped; here the absence IS the outcome, because the
injury report is filed for a specific game and a listed player with no snap row did not play.
Skipping him would have fitted the multipliers on the subset of injured players who turned out to be
fine, which is selection on the outcome and would have produced a multiplier near 1 for every
designation.

### The ablation, at the standard moment: nothing

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6771` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6780` (2023). Three of eight bands improve.

The reason is not the feature. It is arithmetic about when things are published:

| | designations filed | reachable at the standard moment |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 game status | 792 | **5 (0.6%)** |
| 2024 practice status | 1,730 | 17 (1.0%) |
| 2023 game status | 851 | **0 (0.0%)** |
| 2023 practice status | 1,707 | 8 (0.5%) |

The engine replays week W from the moment week W-1's numbers landed — the Tuesday. The league files
its injury reports from the Wednesday, and overwhelmingly on the Friday. **The news layer at the
standard moment is measuring an empty set**, and the small movements in the table are noise from a
handful of rows.

### The ablation, at the game-week moment: the best band result in the project

So the same ablation was run at the latest moment a whole-week replay can honestly use: the morning
of the week's first game. `gameWeekMomentsFor` derives it as thirty-six hours before the first
stored result of the week, which by the stamping arithmetic is noon UTC on the day of that first
game — seven in the morning, Eastern, before any kickoff the NFL schedules. The moment is asserted
in code to be strictly earlier than that week's first stored result, so nothing produced BY the
week's games can pass the gate. At that moment 15-19% of designations are reachable.

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6768` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6776` (2023), with error DOWN on both
(4.577 → 4.575 and 4.461 → **4.456**):

| band | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| elite | 0.0694 → **0.0906** (**+0.0212**) | 0.1649 → **0.1712** (**+0.0063**) |
| starter | 0.1750 → **0.1937** (**+0.0187**) | 0.2209 → **0.2295** (**+0.0087**) |
| deep | 0.3513 → 0.3488 (−0.0025) | 0.3221 → 0.3215 (−0.0006) |
| fringe | 0.2495 → 0.2452 (−0.0044) | 0.2763 → 0.2731 (−0.0032) |

**The two bands where a start-and-sit decision lives improve on both held-out seasons, by the largest
margins any feature in this project has produced, and mean absolute error falls on both.** The
2024 starter band reaches 0.1937, which is above the 0.1919 model v2 reached with all of Phase 3's
context — from a feature reading one in six of the available designations.

Deep and fringe degrade on both seasons, which is also consistent and also makes sense: those bands
are full of players nobody was going to start, and suppressing a questionable one reorders a ranking
that was already about right.

The `before` arm's numbers at the game-week moment are identical to its numbers at the standard
moment, to four decimal places and three decimal places of error. That is the check that this is a
clean A/B: the later moment admits nothing to the model except the news the news layer reads.

### It ships DISABLED, and what would change that

The gate the spec set is that the layer must beat the model on held-out seasons. The pooled rank
correlation is DOWN on both seasons, and two of the four bands are down on both. So it ships off, by
the rule, and the rule is right to be strict about a layer this easy to fall in love with.

But the honest summary is more specific than "it did not work", and the specifics are the result:

1. **At the moment the engine currently replays from, the layer is measuring an empty set.** Nothing
   about the feature has been tested at that moment; only the calendar has.
2. **At the latest moment a WHOLE-WEEK replay can honestly use, it produces the best elite-band and
   starter-band numbers in the project, on both seasons, with error down on both.**
3. **Four fifths of the designations are still out of reach even there**, and no constant can fix it.
   A whole-week moment must precede the week's EARLIEST kickoff, which is Thursday night; the league
   files its decisive designations on the Friday. The fix is not a later moment, it is a **per-player
   moment — his own game's kickoff** — which is a change to `backtest.ts` and not to a number.

That per-player replay moment is the single highest-value item this document produces. It is what a
manager actually faces (lineups lock at kickoff, player by player), it makes four fifths of the
injury wire reachable rather than one fifth, and it is the same change that would let the pre-kickoff
betting line be measured when `capture_snapshots` has collected enough of it. Two features and a
product surface are all waiting on one piece of harness.

## What Phase 3's prediction got right and wrong

Model v2 predicted that teammate-separating features would move the scoreboard, and named four. The
scoreboard after building three of them:

- **Red-zone share: separates teammates, moves nothing.** Confirmed teammate-separating, measured at
  a 31% spread in scoring rate across a 2.6x spread in role, and it does not survive two seasons.
- **Depth of target: separates teammates, and layer 2 had already counted it.** The elasticities are
  real and correctly signed; the residual after layer 2 is about one per cent.
- **Injury designations: the one that works.** And it works on the bands that matter, on both
  seasons.

The refinement to Phase 3's lesson, written down so it can be checked in turn: **it is not enough for
a feature to separate teammates. It has to separate them by a quantity the layers below it have not
already used, and it has to be reachable at the moment the decision is made.** Red-zone share fails
the first test more than it looks (layer 2 is not using touchdown rate, but the leverage itself is
weakly coupled to the outcome). Depth of target fails it outright. The injury wire passes both — and
is then blocked by the third thing nobody had written down, which is that a fact published on Friday
is invisible to an instrument that reads the world on Tuesday.

## What is NOT in model v3

- **Everything model v2 listed as missing**, unchanged: game script, a coordinator table, a
  transaction wire, kickers and team defences.
- **Route participation.** Reachable from `pbp_participation` and deliberately deferred — above.
- **A per-player replay moment.** The most valuable single item on this list.
- **Any live news wire.** The contract is ready for one; the measurement is not, and shipping an
  unmeasurable layer is the thing this phase exists to refuse.

## What to do next, in order

1. **A per-player replay moment**, at each player's own kickoff. It unblocks the news pass, the
   betting line, and the product surface that shows a projection at the moment somebody sets a
   lineup.
2. **Re-measure the news pass there**, with four fifths of the wire reachable instead of one fifth,
   and re-run the gate.
3. **Band by a common prior (ADP)**, still outstanding from model v2, so every band table in this
   document becomes exact rather than indicative.
4. **A thin-history segment for depth of target**, which is the one honest way left to find out
   whether it is worth anything to the players layer 2 knows nothing about.
5. **Route participation**, if and only if items 1 to 4 have not filled the queue.
