# World-Class UX Consistency Review

Full adversarial review of the 8-phase build plan against the strategy doc's own §4/§4.1/§4.2 UX differentiators. See `plans/00-roadmap.md` for the triaged action list this produced.

**Headline:** the plan is engineering-excellent and UX-illiterate. Across ~3,500 lines there are zero occurrences of: dark mode, accessibility, WCAG, screen reader, avatar, GIF, voice, onboarding, design system, animation, motion, haptic, usability, design review, or load-testing-of-the-experience. No phase has a single non-engineering exit criterion. Ten named strategy-doc differentiators were never scheduled as a build task in any of the 8 phases, and the plan never says it dropped them.

---

## CRITICAL

- **C1. No design-system/visual-identity foundation anywhere.** Strategy §4 names a cohesive visual identity — icon system, dark mode, avatars — as "structurally unavailable to Apex." Nothing shared exists; five-plus UI-building steps across multiple phases each invent their own styling. Fix: a design-system foundation (tokens, light+dark palettes together, component primitives with built-in loading/error states, one icon system) that every UI step must read first, the same way `docs/conventions.md` works.
- **C2. Dark mode: named differentiator, never scheduled.** Fix: fold into the design-system foundation — semantic color tokens with light+dark values from day one, a theme provider defaulting to system preference.
- **C3. Accessibility is absent from all 8 phases — a real ADA/legal exposure, not just polish.** Zero hits for WCAG, contrast, keyboard, screen reader. For a real-money product with a 50-state legal surface this is a live exposure on top of being a UX failure. Fix: WCAG 2.2 AA as the stated bar, `eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y` + `axe-core` in the test pipeline, keyboard-only and screen-reader-only completability as named exit criteria on the draft/lineup/join flows, and VoiceOver/TalkBack support in the native apps.
- **C4. Every phase's verification is engineering-only — nothing ever checks "does this feel right."** The plan can pass 100% of its exit criteria and produce something that works perfectly and feels like MyFantasyLeague — the exact outcome this project exists to avoid. Fix: a standing rule that every user-visible-surface step additionally requires a design review, a state-completeness check (loading/empty/error/success all exist, not just happy path), an a11y pass, a copy review, and an interaction-cost check (tap/click count shouldn't regress without justification — the Yahoo lesson from strategy §1.3).
- **C5. No first-time-user onboarding exists — and no league-creation flow is scheduled at all.** The plan assumes total fantasy fluency and, more seriously, **no step in any phase builds a "create a league" UI** — only a backend round-trip test. As planned, a commissioner cannot create a league. Fix: a league-creation flow completable in under 30 seconds via sensible defaults (Sleeper's own benchmark), plus a first-run onboarding fork (experience-level branching, invite mechanism, empty-state coaching).
- **C6. Dynasty support — a top-3 named differentiator — is silently abandoned.** Strategy §4's first principle is "one draft engine, every format... snake, auction, and dynasty." The plan explicitly builds only snake+auction and never revisits dynasty in any of the 8 phases. Fix: either an honest deferral note (marketing must not claim "every format" at launch) or a real Phase 9 covering dynasty/keeper mechanics, rookie-pick trading, and an orphan-team marketplace.
- **C7. The unfiltered embedded review feed is never scheduled**, despite being named three separate times in the strategy doc as a direct trust-signal counter to Apex's curated testimonials page. Fix: an in-product review feed, sorted newest-first, no editorial filter on rating, moderation limited to spam/abuse with the removal itself logged.
- **C8. Money-handling UX (Phase 4) is pure ledger engineering with a UI bolted on.** The highest-anxiety moment in the product gets a few bullets. No balance display is designed anywhere (total/withdrawable/escrow/held-for-review), the "always-on escrow indicator" strategy §4 requires is reduced to a per-contest afterthought, only one withdrawal method is ever specified (directly contradicting the stated goal of beating Underdog's "no credit-card withdrawal" complaint), and staged KYC is a backend gate with no designed flow explaining to the user why it's happening. Fix: a dedicated wallet/trust-UX pass — persistent balance surface with plain-language breakdowns, a global escrow indicator, multiple withdrawal methods with fee/ETA shown before commit, and a KYC flow with a "why we're asking" explanation and a real rejection/appeal path.
- **C9. State-blocked, withholding, and 1099 delivery are undesigned failure moments (Phase 5).** "Your state isn't supported" is a typed exception, not a designed moment; 24% withholding has zero requirement to explain itself to the user who just got a smaller-than-expected payout; nothing in the plan ever shows a user their own 1099 (the dashboard that generates them is explicitly internal-only). Fix: an honest state-ineligibility screen with a waitlist, pre-emptive eligibility checks before a user builds a roster they can't enter, withholding transparency on every payout confirmation, and a user-facing Tax Center for TIN status and downloadable forms.

## IMPORTANT

- **I1. Loading/error/empty states are never systematically required** — "empty state" appears exactly once in the entire plan (Phase 2's zero-leagues case, which is genuinely well done and should be the template copied everywhere else).
- **I2. GIFs and voice in chat — named four times in the strategy doc — quietly reduced to text + emoji reactions** with no later phase ever picking the deferred scope back up.
- **I3. Avatar/profile customization and mascots never scheduled** — the cheapest identity/delight lever available in a social product where users stare at opponents' names all season.
- **I4. Draft-party TV casting — named twice — never scheduled**, despite being one of the highest social-proof, lowest-cost differentiators in the doc.
- **I5. Auto-tightening slow-draft clocks — a named §4 principle solving Apex's own worst weakness — never built.** The draft engine implements only a flat timer.
- **I6. The AI layer is metered, secured, and evaluated (genuinely strong work) but never designed as a conversation** — no streaming, no latency-perception design, a degraded state that's a single string, no designed output rendering for a recommendation.
- **I7. Native apps aim at feature parity, not platform-native feel** — no HIG/Material guidance, no haptics anywhere in a product whose whole premise is tactile anticipation, no Live Activities/Dynamic Island (a near-perfect, Apex-unavailable fit for "you're on the clock").
- **I8. Zero moments of delight, celebration, or motion design** beyond the one genuinely strong piece (optimistic UI). No draft-pick landing animation, no matchup-win celebration, no payout-confirmed moment — the screen the whole season builds to.
- **I9. Peak-Sunday load testing — a named non-functional requirement in strategy §5 — is never scheduled** (also flagged independently by the scaling review).
- **I10. No responsive/mobile-web requirement in Phases 1–6**, despite the strategy explicitly noting real-money users "check lineups compulsively during games" — every user before Phase 7 ships is on a desktop-first draft board on their phone.

## MINOR

- AI weekly recaps/trash-talk headlines (named as "the highest delight-per-token feature in the doc" by the strategy itself) never scheduled.
- Integrity actions (auto-set lineups, held trades, tier-based blocks) are invisible to the users they affect — no user-facing explanation designed for any of them.
- No UI/UX counterpart to `docs/conventions.md` — every UI step reinvents its own approach.
- No error-message copy standard — errors are specified as typed exceptions with no required human-readable mapping.
- The commissioner bounty (a named, unique monetization lever) never scheduled — worth an explicit deferral note rather than silence.

## Already well-handled

Optimistic UI (the plan's strongest UX thinking, carried correctly into both native clients). Contextual push notifications (Phase 7 gets this exactly right — typed events, config-driven, no blast-style default). Degrade-gracefully-without-AI (enforced with tests, not just described). Phase 2's zero-leagues empty state (the template the rest of the plan should copy). Phase 7's reconnect UX ("reconnecting…" state, network-quality indicator) — the right instinct, applied in only one of fifty steps.
