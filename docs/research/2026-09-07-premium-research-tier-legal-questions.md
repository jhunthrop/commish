# Premium research tier: the rule and the questions for counsel

**Founder ruling, 2026-09-07.** Paid features are research products only. Contests are identical for every manager: the same pot, rake, payouts, waiver order, roster rules, pick timing and draft tools, whether or not anyone in the league subscribes. A premium feature is information a manager could obtain elsewhere (projections, written analysis, tools, history), never a mechanic.

This document is not legal advice. It is the product's own rule plus the questions to put to counsel before real money moves. The rake itself is the regulated activity; the subscription rides on the posture that work establishes (see the phase 8 legal launch readiness plan and the LLC filing).

## The product rule, testable

1. No paid feature reads or writes contest state in a way a free account cannot: no extra roster slots, no waiver priority, no pick-clock changes, no trade review weight, no payout difference.
2. The pot, the rake percentage and the payout table are fixed before the season and identical for subscribers and non-subscribers, and are shown to every manager in the same words.
3. Premium copy says what a manager will know, never what he will win. "Win more" style claims are banned vocabulary in premium marketing, the same way competitor names are banned in product copy.
4. Every premium surface is labeled as research and shows its basis (the same both-bases discipline the dossier uses).
5. A guard test in the repo asserts that no premium-gated code path touches the contest tables (rosters, waivers, drafts, payouts, matchups). Ledger this as a follow-up for the money round.

## Questions for counsel

1. **Entry states.** Which states will we accept paid entries from at launch, which require operator registration or licensing for paid fantasy (and their fees, bonding and audit requirements), and which do we exclude? Does taking a rake rather than a fixed entry fee change the analysis anywhere?
2. **Federal carve-out fit.** Confirm the contest design meets the UIGEA fantasy conditions as we run it: outcomes reflect knowledge and skill, prizes and pot are established before entry and not determined by the number of entrants, and no result depends on a single real-world game or one athlete's performance. The house-league factory sets pots per tier in advance; confirm the "never empty lobby" mechanism does not conflict with "prizes established in advance".
3. **Subscription neutrality language.** Terms of service wording that states the premium tier is research only and confers no contest advantage, and whether any state fantasy statute requires an explicit disclosure of that.
4. **Auto-renewal and cancellation.** Requirements for the subscription checkout and cancellation flow under California's and other states' automatic renewal laws: consent capture, renewal notice, one-click cancellation, price-change notice.
5. **Fee disclosures.** Where the rake percentage, the pot and the payout table must appear (league creation, join flow, terms), and whether any state requires disclosure of the operator's fee separately from the subscription price.
6. **Bundling.** Is there any issue with offering the subscription at a discount to managers in paid leagues, or bundling it with an entry fee, or must the two remain separately priced and separately cancellable?
7. **Age and identity.** Age verification and identity checks required for paid entries in the states we accept, and whether the subscription alone (no entry) triggers any of them.
8. **Escrow and payouts.** Handling of entry fees held for the season (segregation, escrow, refunds on league abandonment) under the states' rules, and tax reporting thresholds for prizes.
9. **Advertising.** Any state restrictions on how paid fantasy contests and premium tools may be advertised, including to minors and in states we exclude.
10. **Data and AI.** Whether written analysis generated by a model needs a disclosure, and any consumer-protection exposure from analysis presented as expert opinion.

## Added 2026-10-15: moves made on a host with the member's stored session

The in-season tools will set a lineup or submit a waiver claim on the member's host when the member confirms the exact move on our screen, using the session the member stored with us (ESPN's two cookies, FFPC's sign-in, MyFantasyLeague's login cookie). Each host's terms bar automated access in some words (Disney's terms bar robots and scrapers; see `docs/research/2026-10-15-host-writes-design.md` §3 for the quotes and links). Questions for counsel: whether one human-confirmed move sent from our server on the member's behalf, with the member's own session, is "automated access" under each host's terms; the account-level consequence to the member if a host objects, and what we must tell the member before they store a session; whether a written notice to each host, or a partner feed where one exists, changes the answer. The founder accepted the FFPC risk on 2026-10-13; the same acceptance is needed per host before its write switch turns on.
