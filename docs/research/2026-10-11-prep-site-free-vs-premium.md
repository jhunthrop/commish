# Prep site: free versus premium

**Date:** 2026-10-11. **Status:** research for the pivot to a draft prep site ([pivot memory](../../../../.claude/projects/-Users-jh-code-fantasy/memory/pivot-draft-prep-site.md)). This is an internal doc, so competitors are named here. Product copy still never names them.

**Method.** I read public pages only. No sign-ups, no checkouts. All prices were observed on 2026-10-11, which is mid-season, so several sites show struck-through "sale" prices. Both numbers are recorded where shown. Flock Fantasy's site is a client-rendered app with no readable pricing page, so its prices come from the public, unauthenticated product endpoints its own pricing page calls ([S2]). Each bracketed tag points to the source list at the end. Lines marked *reasoning* are my analysis, not something I found.

### Public evidence on subscriber counts

Nobody publishes paid subscriber counts. What exists is self-reported reach:

The only industry denominator I found is FSGA's 2025 study: about 53 million US fantasy players [S16]. The only conversion benchmarks I found are general ones. Freemium software converts roughly 3 to 5% of users [S17]. RevenueCat reports, via a secondary summary, 2.1% download-to-paid by day 35 for freemium apps versus 10.7% for hard paywalls [S18]. Treat both as priors, not fantasy-specific facts.

## 2. What the market has settled on as free versus paid

**Settled as free:**

- **Consensus rankings and basic ADP.** FantasyPros' identity is the free consensus. Underdog ADP is republished free by 4for4, FTN, Sharp and others [S4b, S14]. Underdog's own rankings tab exports CSV [S14].
- **News, articles, podcasts.** Fantasy Life gives away "all written, video and audio content" [S10]. USA TODAY keeps news, injuries and stats free [S11].
- **Mock drafts at standard settings.** FantasyPros' simulator is free for standard 10/12-team scoring. Custom league settings are the paid line [S1b].
- **Trying a tool once on your own league.** Footballguys offers a free league-sync preview of Draft Dominator [S3]. An old FantasyPros page gave "1 free draft analysis" [S1b].

**Settled as paid:**

- **The assistant during a real draft, with live sync.** It is the tier divider at FantasyPros (MVP and up) [S1], 4for4 (Pro, not Lite) [S4], Footballguys (PRO) [S3] and PFF [S8b]. FantasyPros even writes its refund policy around it: multiple Draft Assistant uses void the refund [S1d]. That is the clearest public evidence of which feature the market treats as the product.
- **Rankings and values for your league's custom scoring.** Footballguys sells "rankings tailored to your league" [S3]. FantasyPros sells "Custom Mock Drafts & Cheat Sheets" in PRO [S1]. Fantasy Life puts cheat sheets and tiers in Tier 1 [S10].
- **Deeper projections and data.** Projections are paid at 4for4 [S4], RotoViz [S6] and Fantasy Life Tier 2 [S10]. Stats and grades are paid at PFF [S8].
- **In-season tools.** Start/sit, waivers, trade analysis and lineup autopilot carry the higher tiers and the monthly billing options at FantasyPros, ETR, Footballguys and USA TODAY [S1, S3, S5, S11].
- **Format breadth.** Dynasty, keeper, best ball, IDP and auction tools are the upsell from base to the next tier [S1, S3, S4].
- **Community.** Discord is a named paid perk at Flock (every tier), Footballguys All-Access, ETR and 4for4 [S2, S3, S4, S5].
- **Ad removal.** "No ads" is in 4for4 Lite [S4]. USA TODAY sells a "reduced ad experience" [S11].

**Three packaging patterns worth noting:**

- **Draft-only prices sit at about $30 to $60 a season.** Examples: 4for4 Lite $29 to $39, Flock Season Pass $34 to $49, FantasyPros PRO $47.88/yr, ETR Draft Kit Pro $54.99, Footballguys PRO $59.99 [S1–S5].
- **Full-year memberships with in-season tools sit at about $90 to $140.** Examples: Footballguys ELITE $89.99, PFF $99.99, Fantasy Life Tier 2 $99.99, FantasyPros HOF $107.88, Flock Flocker $119, RotoViz $129.99 [S2, S3, S6, S8, S10].
- **Monthly prices are set to push buyers to annual.** FantasyPros PRO is $11.99 monthly versus $3.99 annual-equivalent, and it says "Save 60%" [S1].

**Two practices Commish cannot copy under its rules.** First, "win more" claims: FantasyPros' "double the chances of winning," Footballguys' "Dominate," ETR's playoff rate [S1, S3, S5]. Second, subscriptions given free for a sportsbook or sweepstakes deposit (FantasyPros, 4for4, DLF) [S1, S4, S7]. Footballguys also bundles entry into a prize contest with paid plans [S3]. Commish must not do that at all (section 5).

### What only Commish has

Three assets have no priced equivalent because no competitor offers them:

- **Grounded prose for every player.** Experts write a few hundred capsules at most.
- **Survival odds:** "he is 34% to be there at your next pick."
- **Prospectively registered accuracy.**

*Reasoning:* nobody charges for these, so there is no market price to anchor on. The right move is to use the prose and the accuracy record to bring people in, and to charge where the market has already trained people to pay: the assistant in a real draft. Survival odds are what make that assistant visibly different.

## 4. Three candidate models

All three keep the same free floor. Under each model, the free tier must be worth opening every day in August. *Reasoning:* that means the free board, today's ADP movement, every player page with its prose, unlimited bot mocks and a saved cheat sheet. If the free tier is thin, the accuracy story never gets an audience.

### (a) Draft Pass, a one-time charge per season

- **Free:** everything in the floor, plus the advisor and survival odds inside Commish mocks.
- **Paid, $39 per draft season ($29 before July 1):** the advisor in your real drafts (sync or fast manual entry), custom-scoring boards and projections, unlimited leagues, CSV export of custom boards. Access runs through the season's end.
- **Why:** the price sits in the observed draft-only band of $29 to $59 [S1–S5]. It matches what Commish actually has after the pivot, which is no in-season product. 4for4 and Flock already sell per-season access [S2, S4]. A non-renewing charge also avoids most auto-renewal mechanics (a counsel question, section 5).
- **Weakness:** revenue lands in one five-week window, and there is no renewal compounding.

### (b) Annual membership

- **Free:** the same floor.
- **Paid, $59/yr (or $8.99/mo):** everything in (a), plus whatever in-season research ships, such as rest-of-season boards and weekly projections with basis.
- **Why:** this is the dominant packaging in the table [S1, S3, S8, S10].
- **Weakness:** the pivot dropped Sunday and in-season work ([pivot memory](../../../../.claude/projects/-Users-jh-code-fantasy/memory/pivot-draft-prep-site.md)). Selling a 12-month product with a 2-month product inside it invites refund requests and churn. Footballguys' 30-day guarantee [S3] and FantasyPros' refund carve-out [S1d] show that this abuse pattern exists.

### (c) Free everything plus one paid Advisor

- **Free:** every board including custom scoring, projections, prose, mocks, cheat sheet and export.
- **Paid, $29 per season:** "Advisor," the live real-draft assistant with survival odds, nothing else.
- **Why:** it puts the paywall exactly where the market's own refund policy shows the value is [S1d]. It is the simplest offer to explain, and it is the most generous free tier in the category.
- **Weakness:** one paid feature at $29 needs more volume. At the 2 to 5% conversion priors [S17, S18], 100,000 August users convert to roughly 2,000 to 5,000 buyers, which is $58k to $145k. That is my arithmetic on general benchmarks, not a forecast.

## 5. Legal and operational constraints that carry over

From the [premium-is-research-only ruling](../../../../.claude/projects/-Users-jh-code-fantasy/memory/premium-is-research-only.md) and the [counsel questions](2026-09-07-premium-research-tier-legal-questions.md):

1. **No money at stake, no contests.** The pivot removes the rake and the leagues. The rule's spirit still binds: paid is information only. That rules out a subscriber prize contest like Footballguys' [S3] and deposit-for-subscription partner promotions like FantasyPros', 4for4's and DLF's [S1, S4, S7]. Those promotions tie the product to gambling-operator marketing, which counsel question 9 (advertising) already covers. *Recommend we decide no, as a product rule, before counsel is asked.*
2. **Copy says what you will know, never what you will win.** This bans the category's most common pitch [S1, S3, S5]. The held-out accuracy record is the lawful substitute, because it describes the projections, not the buyer's results.
3. **Subscriptions or passes only, through a standard processor.** Flock runs on Stripe (its product and price IDs are Stripe objects [S2]). Under the pivot, a research subscription is ordinary digital goods for a processor. *Question for counsel and the processor:* does fantasy-adjacent content trigger any restricted-business review now that nothing is wagered?
4. **Auto-renewal law (counsel question 4).** If Commish auto-renews, the California-style consent, renewal notice and online cancellation requirements apply. RotoViz's "all subscriptions automatically renew" disclosure [S6] and USA TODAY's renewal language [S11] are the market norm. *Question:* does a non-renewing season pass (model a or c) fall outside these statutes entirely?
5. **Refunds.** The market splits three ways: 30 days no questions (Footballguys [S3]), conditional (FantasyPros [S1d]), and final sale (4for4, Fantasy Life [S4, S10]). *Question for counsel:* is "final sale after first real-draft use" enforceable and consistent with state consumer law and processor chargeback rules?
6. **State sales tax on digital subscriptions.** States differ on whether digital information services are taxable. USA TODAY prices "exclude taxes" [S11]. *This is a question for counsel and an accountant, not an answer here:* where does Commish have nexus, and do research subscriptions count as taxable digital products in those states?
7. **AI disclosure (counsel question 10).** The grounded prose is model-written. That question carries forward unchanged.
8. **App stores.** FantasyPros notes app-store purchases follow store refund rules [S1d], and the stores take a commission. *Reasoning:* a web-only checkout keeps refunds and margin in Commish's hands.

Counsel questions 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 (entry states, UIGEA, fee disclosure, bundling with entries, age checks for entries, escrow) fall away with the pivot. Questions 3, 4, 9 and 10 survive, reworded for a pure research product.

## 6. Recommendation

**Ship model (c) with a small step toward (a): free everything, plus one paid "Draft Pass."**

- **Free:** the board for every preset format, projections, every player page with its prose, ADP, search, unlimited bot mocks with the advisor and survival odds inside mocks, the cheat sheet with CSV, and the public accuracy register.
- **Paid, $29 per draft season, non-renewing, web checkout only:** the advisor and survival odds in your real drafts, plus custom-scoring boards.

**Reasoning:**

1. The market's paid line is the real-draft assistant. FantasyPros' refund carve-out is the clearest evidence of it [S1, S1d].
2. Commish has no in-season product, so annual packaging overstates what it sells.
3. $29 is the bottom of the observed draft-only band [S4], which suits a first-year brand without recognizable experts.
4. A free tier richer than anyone's is what makes the accuracy claim and the prose reach people in August. Both are assets nobody else prices.
5. A non-renewing pass likely removes the auto-renewal compliance work, pending counsel.

Custom scoring stays paid only because the veteran persona will pay for it while the casual does not need it. If commissioners ask for it, it moves to free.

**Numbers that would change this:**

- **The 2026 register fails to beat consensus.** Then the accuracy claim is gone. Raise the free tier further and rely on the advisor alone, or reconsider whether to charge in year one.
- **Measured free-to-paid conversion above about 6%,** above the general priors [S17]. That signals underpricing. Test $39, which is the 4for4 list price [S4].
- **The share of payers drafting three or more leagues.** If it is high, add a $49 multi-league tier rather than raising the base price.
- **A real in-season product ships.** Then move to model (b) at $59 to $69/yr, inside the $48 to $108 annual band [S1, S3].
- **LLM cost per player page and per refresh.** If free prose costs more per August visitor than conversion pays back, keep the capsule free and gate refresh frequency, not the prose itself.
- **Real-draft sync with ESPN, Yahoo and Sleeper is not buildable by 2027.** Without sync the paid line weakens to manual entry. Then price at $19 or bundle custom scoring with weekly board updates instead.
