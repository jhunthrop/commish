# Model v2, feature by feature

**Measured 2026-08-28.** Reproduce with `npm run projections:backfill 2020 2024`,
`npm run projections:context 2016 2024`, `npm run projections:fit`, then
`npm run projections:ablate <group>` for any of `line`, `scheme`, `redistribution`, `context`,
`shipped`, `shares`.
Spec: `docs/design/2026-08-27-projection-engine-spec.md`. Previous: `docs/research/2026-08-27-model-v1.md`.

Phase 2 built a usage engine and measured it. Phase 3 added the context that is supposed to explain
why usage moves, one group at a time, each ablated on two seasons no fit has ever seen. **Two of the
four earned their place and two did not**, and the two that did not are the more interesting result.

## The scoreboard

Model v1 against model v2, like for like, PPR, both held-out seasons.

| | 2024 rho | 2024 MAE | 2023 rho | 2023 MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive floor | 0.5690 | 5.120 | 0.5613 | 5.062 |
| model v1 | 0.6770 | 4.577 | 0.6780 | 4.461 |
| **model v2** | **0.6767** | **4.578** | **0.6789** | **4.459** |

**The headline number did not move, and that is the correct summary of the phase.** What moved is
underneath it:

| band | 2024 floor | v1 | v2 | Δ | 2023 floor | v1 | v2 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| elite (1–50) | 0.0452 | 0.0694 | **0.0889** | **+0.0195** | 0.1536 | 0.1649 | **0.1905** | **+0.0257** |
| starter (51–150) | 0.2917 | 0.1750 | **0.1919** | **+0.0169** | 0.0867 | 0.2209 | **0.2308** | **+0.0100** |
| deep (151–400) | 0.2645 | 0.3513 | 0.3568 | +0.0055 | 0.1813 | 0.3221 | 0.3288 | +0.0067 |
| fringe (401+) | 0.1856 | 0.2495 | 0.2469 | −0.0026 | 0.0678 | 0.2763 | 0.2860 | +0.0097 |

Seven of eight bands improve. The two that improve most are the two where a manager's weekly
decision lives.

**Why the pooled number can be flat while every band rises.** Pooled rank correlation over four
thousand player-weeks is dominated by ordering *across* bands, and everybody gets that right,
including the naive floor: a starting receiver outranks a backup tight end. Within a band the range
is restricted and the ordering is genuinely hard. A feature can therefore be worth having and leave
the headline untouched. The plan's own self-review warned about exactly this ("report per-segment,
not just the average, or you will discard the work for moving the overall number by 0.004"), and
this phase is the case it was warning about.

**The caveat, because it is real.** Bands are assigned by each source's own projected rank, so the
two arms do not hold identical players (695 against 697 in the 2024 elite band). A band-to-band
comparison is indicative rather than exact. Seven consistent signs across two independent seasons is
strong evidence, not proof. Banding by a common prior (ADP) would settle it and remains on the list.

### The starter band, which model v1 named as its one regression

v1 lost the 2024 starter band to the floor, 0.1750 against 0.2917, and said so in bold. v2 narrows
that to 0.1919 against 0.2917 — **the gap closes by about a seventh and the band is still behind the
floor in 2024**. It was never behind in 2023 (0.2308 against 0.0867) and it still is not. So the
2024 starter regression has now survived a second look, is smaller, and remains the single clearest
open defect in the engine.

## Feature by feature, including what failed

### 1. The offensive line composite — NOT SHIPPED

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6772` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6780` (2023).

Adjusted line yards on the conventional yardage bands, stuffed rate and sack rate allowed from
play-by-play; continuity from snap participation. No PFF at runtime, per the founder ruling of
2026-08-27. Continuity is used as a *confidence* rather than as a fourth component: an unsettled line
is not a bad line, it is an unknown one, and the honest response to the rates it posted is to pull
them toward the league rather than to treat instability as badness.

The bands moved in opposite directions on the two seasons and the overall figure did not move at
all. Four reasons, in the order the diagnosis went:

1. **The composite's own signal is tiny.** On the fit seasons it correlates with same-week team
   rushing efficiency at rho 0.052. Component by component: adjusted line yards 0.084, stuffed rate
   −0.025, sack rate against passing efficiency −0.082. Every sign is right and every magnitude is
   near zero.
2. **The window mattered more than the feature did, and finding that out was worth the task.** Built
   across the offseason the composite correlates at 0.024; built from the current season alone,
   0.052, and the fitted elasticity moves from **−0.013 to +0.185**. An offensive line is
   substantially rebuilt every March and last January is nearly no evidence about this October. This
   is a reusable lesson for any future personnel-derived feature.
3. Even at elasticity 0.185, a line 15% better than average buys 2.6% more rushing yards per carry,
   on a rate layer 2 already regresses toward its role at 88 carries of prior.
4. **The structural reason, and the one to remember: it is a team-level multiplier and the
   scoreboard is a ranking.** Every player on an offence moves together by the same 2.6%, so almost
   nothing is reordered.

The code and the ingestion stay. The ingestion is the expensive part and it feeds groups 2 and 4.

### 2. Scheme, pace and the coaching-change prior — SHIPPED

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6767` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6781` (2023), with **eight rank bands out of eight
improving**:

| band | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| elite | 0.0694 → 0.0817 (+0.0123) | 0.1649 → 0.1887 (+0.0239) |
| starter | 0.1750 → 0.1890 (+0.0140) | 0.2209 → 0.2276 (+0.0067) |
| deep | 0.3513 → 0.3562 (+0.0049) | 0.3221 → 0.3279 (+0.0058) |
| fringe | 0.2495 → 0.2543 (+0.0047) | 0.2763 → 0.2871 (+0.0108) |

Situation-neutral pace and pass rate, measured with win probability between 0.2 and 0.8, outside the
two-minute drill, in regulation — the only window in which what an offence does is what it *intends*
to do. Plus nflfastR's own pass rate over expected, which we take rather than re-derive. The claim
is that layer 1's realised team volumes are contaminated by game script and these two are not.

Fitted against the **residual** of layer 1's own team-volume prediction, never against raw volume:
layer 1 already predicts volume from realised volume, which is mostly a function of pace, and
fitting there would count the same effect twice. Every sign is the one theory demands:

| volume | tempo | pass lean |
|---|---|---|
| team targets | +0.107 | +0.205 |
| team carries | +0.100 | −0.125 |
| team pass attempts | +0.028 | +0.228 |

Absolute error rose by about 0.15%, which is the honest cost of the band improvements.

**The coaching change.** A team's tendencies are its play-caller's, so when he leaves, last season
stops being evidence about this one. A new coach's team regresses toward *his* pace and pass rate
from the offences he ran elsewhere; a returning coach's regresses toward the team's own; a
first-time coach falls back to the league, never to the man he replaced. Six to eleven teams a
season are affected, which is the real rate of head-coaching turnover.

**The table is sourced, not curated, and that is a deliberate departure from the plan.** The plan
asked for a hand-maintained play-caller table, thirty-two rows a year. A table typed from memory is
a table of unverifiable claims, and a wrong coordinator in 2022 is a wrong prior that nothing
downstream can detect — the same standard the news pass will be held to. We ship the **head coach**,
which nflverse's `games.csv` carries for every game back to 1999 from a source we can point at. The
cost is stated plainly: where a coach keeps his job and replaces his coordinator, this sees nothing,
and that is the commonest play-calling change there is. The mechanism is indifferent to which name
it is given, so a sourced coordinator table drops straight in.

### 3. Opportunity redistribution — NOT SHIPPED

Measured on the **post-transaction segment** as the plan required, not on the average:

`rho 0.6837 -> 0.6840` (2024, n=2,698) and `0.6851 -> 0.6840` (2023, n=2,396).

Opposite signs on two independent seasons, on exactly the player-weeks the feature was built for.
This was the founder's own example and the group this phase expected most from.

**The finding is worth more than the feature would have been: layer 1 already does this.** The
absorption rate — how much of a departed player's share the remaining roster takes on *beyond* what
layer 1 already predicts for them — measures **0.065 over 796 team-weeks** with a detected departure
on the fit seasons. Swept across every sensible detection setting (one to four team games of
relevance, 6 / 10 / 15% minimum vacated share) **it never exceeded 0.09**. Roughly nine tenths of the
redistribution is already in the model.

The mechanism was there all along and had simply never been named as the thing doing this job: a
departed player stops appearing in the usage history, his teammates' in-season recency-weighted
shares rise on their own within a game or two, and the prior-season level is shrunk hard enough not
to fight the new evidence.

Two detection bugs were found and fixed on the way, both of which would have made the ablation a lie
and both of which are the kind that produce a plausible number:

- Without a floor on the vacated share the detector fired in **1,080 of 1,152 team-weeks** (a fifth
  receiver who took 1% of the targets is a departure by every structural test) and the estimated
  absorption collapsed to 0.015 against a thousand non-events.
- The relevance window originally asked whether the departing player appeared in the team's last few
  games. A departure is defined by *absence* from those games, so the test could never fire for a
  release or an injury and had quietly reduced the whole feature to trades.

The specification test is the Amari Cooper trade of October 2024 — Cleveland to Buffalo before week
7, with Jerry Jeudy absorbing most of a 21% target share, Cedric Tillman some, David Njoku less and
the back almost none. It passes. The feature does what it was asked to do; the model already did it.

**Where this would pay: a transaction wire, not better arithmetic.** Every number above is bounded
by the fact that this engine learns of a trade one Sunday late — it reads no transaction feed, so
the earliest honest moment is the snap a man takes for somebody else. A feed that said so on the
Tuesday would move the whole effect into the week where it is worth something. That is an ingestion
problem, not a modelling one.

### 4. Game context — SHIPPED

`rho 0.6770 -> 0.6770` (2024) and `0.6780 -> 0.6789` (2023), with error down on both:

| band | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| elite | 0.0694 → 0.0795 (+0.0102) | 0.1649 → 0.1751 (+0.0102) |
| starter | 0.1750 → 0.1823 (+0.0073) | 0.2209 → 0.2267 (+0.0058) |
| deep | 0.3513 → 0.3549 (+0.0036) | 0.3221 → 0.3213 (−0.0008) |
| fringe | 0.2495 → 0.2500 (+0.0005) | 0.2763 → 0.2742 (−0.0021) |

The elite band improves by 0.0102 on both seasons, to four decimal places. Mean absolute error falls
on both (4.577 → 4.575 and 4.461 → 4.451). Deep and fringe are neutral, which is what one expects:
the opponent matters most to the players who see enough of the ball for a matchup to show.

**The opponent adjustment is the feature.** A defence that has faced four bad offences is not an
elite defence, and every unadjusted points-allowed table in fantasy football says it is. Each
defence's allowed yards per dropback and per carry are divided by the strength of the offences that
produced them, shrunk six team-games toward the league, and computed from results strictly before
the week being rated. **It carries the largest elasticity found anywhere in this phase: 0.42 on
passing and 0.59 on rushing**, against the line composite's 0.185.

Rest is included and is knowable, because the schedule is published in May. Its elasticity is small
and slightly negative, which is counterintuitive, close to zero, and not being explained away here.

## What was asked for and could not be measured

**Implied team total, spread and weather.** nflverse's `games.csv` carries all of them and the
betting line is probably the single most valuable context feature in fantasy football. They are
**closing** lines and **recorded** temperatures, which become knowable at kickoff. A week-W
projection is replayed from the moment week W-1's numbers landed, so it cannot reach them.

They are ingested anyway, under a separate `nflverse_kickoff` source stamp, and they sit in the
store unreachable. That is deliberate: stamping a closing line as though it had been knowable on the
Tuesday would make the feature measure beautifully and make every number this harness has ever
produced a lie. The store's `as_of` column is the only place the difference between "knowable in
May" and "knowable at kickoff" is recorded, and it is doing exactly the job it was built for.

**The fix is already running.** `capture_snapshots` polls Tank01's betting odds and stamps them when
we fetched them. In a season or two that table will hold genuine pre-kickoff lines with honest
stamps, and game script can be measured properly. Until then the answer is a bounded, reported "no"
rather than a number nobody could trust.

## The Phase 2 open item: the fit objective

Model v1 identified one clear defect — the fit's objective covered three of the four shares the
model emits, and pass-attempt share, the one left out, was the one whose held-out rank correlation
came in below the floor (0.496 against 0.858). It was deliberately not fixed there, because fixing
it after reading held-out numbers is tuning against the test set.

Fixed here, and **it changes nothing**. With all four shares in the objective the grid lands on
exactly the point it landed on before (decay 0.7, season shrinkage 2, in-season 0.25) and every
fitted parameter is byte-identical. Layer 1's pass-attempt share still measures 0.4961 in 2024 and
0.4984 in 2023.

So the objective defect was **not** the cause. The other cause v1 named is the whole of it: actual
pass-attempt share is exactly 0 or exactly 1 for almost everybody, the floor reproduces those exact
values and inherits the tie structure, and the model emits 0.85 where the actual is 1.00 and breaks
ties the actuals do not have. Rank correlation punishes inventing an ordering nobody claimed. On
RMSE, where no tie structure exists, the model already wins on all four shares.

Snapping small predictions to zero would recover the ties and the rank correlation. That is gaming
the instrument rather than improving the forecast, and it is not done. The objective change stays
regardless: a model that emits four numbers while being scored on three is being told one of its
outputs does not matter.

## The decay curve

The naive floor decays through a season because it never stops reading January. Whether a model
flattens that curve is the direct test of whether its in-season features do anything.

| | weeks 1–9 | weeks 10–18 | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| floor, 2024 | 0.5868 | 0.5546 | decays |
| model v1, 2024 | 0.6576 | 0.7035 | improves |
| **model v2, 2024** | **0.6576** | **0.7030** | **improves** |
| floor, 2023 | 0.5814 | 0.5421 | decays |
| model v1, 2023 | 0.6762 | 0.6824 | flat |
| **model v2, 2023** | **0.6775** | **0.6832** | **flat, slightly up** |

**Phase 3 did not flatten the curve further, and on reflection it was never going to.** The curve is
about how quickly a model reads the current season, and v1 already reverses it. Every group added
here is a *cross-sectional* correction — who you play, how fast you play — rather than a faster
read of the season.

(These half-season figures are the **like-for-like** ones, restricted to the players both sources
projected. Model v1's own document reported the unrestricted numbers for the same weeks — 0.669 and
0.710 in 2024 — so compare within this table, not across the two documents.)

The place still to be won is the left-hand end. Week 1: v2 gains +0.0005 on v1 in 2024 and +0.0012 in
2023, and **still loses week 1 to the floor in 2024** (0.6787 against 0.6819), while winning it in
2023 (0.5852 against 0.5749). With no current-season evidence layer 1 collapses to a prior-season
mean, which is very nearly what the naive floor already is, and none of Phase 3's context is enough
to break the tie. **Draft-day projections remain a different problem needing different inputs.** Week
18 is worst for both, for the reason Phase 1 gave: playoff teams rest starters, which is forecastable
and which nothing here forecasts.

## The lesson this phase actually taught

Three of the four groups are **team-level multipliers**: the line, the scheme, the opponent. Every
player on an offence moves together, so the ordering *within* a team is untouched and the ordering
*between* teams moves only a little. That is why they produce band-level improvements of 0.01 to
0.02 and a pooled number that does not move.

The one group that moved players relative to their own teammates — redistribution — turned out to be
something layer 1 already did.

So the prediction for Phase 4 and beyond, written down here so it can be checked: **the features
that will move this scoreboard are the ones that separate teammates from each other.** Red-zone
share, route participation, target depth, and the injury and inactive designations that decide who
is on the field at all. Not more team-level context; that seam is close to worked out.

## What is NOT in model v2

- **Game script.** Above. Needs pre-kickoff capture, which is running.
- **A coordinator table.** We ship head coaches because they are sourced. Play-calling changes under
  a retained head coach are invisible.
- **Route participation and red-zone share.** Still no free source for routes; red-zone share is now
  *reachable*, because play-by-play is ingested, and it is the largest remaining gap — the one thing
  that would make touchdown rate partly predictable rather than entirely regressed.
- **A transaction wire.** The redistribution result is bounded by learning of trades one Sunday late.
- **Kickers and team defences.** Unchanged from v1.

## What to do next, in order

1. **Red-zone share, from the play-by-play that is now ingested.** It separates teammates, which is
   the property this phase's evidence says matters, and it is the only route to a partly predictable
   touchdown rate.
2. **Cut error in the 2024 starter band.** Narrowed from −0.117 to −0.100 against the floor and still
   the clearest open defect. It has now survived two independent looks.
3. **Band by a common prior (ADP)** so every band comparison in this document becomes exact rather
   than indicative. Half the conclusions here rest on band-level numbers.
4. **Pre-kickoff odds capture**, then re-measure game script. The ingestion is running; the wait is
   calendar time.
5. **Phase 4, the news pass**, under the gate the spec set: it must beat this model on held-out
   seasons or it ships disabled. Two of four groups failed that test in this phase, which is the
   evidence that the gate works.
